Commentary

This study refuses to treat those who leave and those who protest as separate groups. It starts from an insight by Albert O. Hirschman: exit and voice can reinforce rather than exclude each other. Applied to young Moroccans in 2025–2026, this lens sheds light on the wish to leave, the GenZ 212 mobilisation and falling electoral turnout alike.

Key takeaways

  1. In the third quarter of 2026, unemployment among 15–24-year-olds reached 30.5%, nearly three times the national average; 2.9 million people aged 15–29 are not in employment, education or training.
  2. 64% of 18–35-year-olds have considered emigrating, 28% “a lot”; Europe remains the preferred destination.
  3. GenZ 212 put health, education and the fight against corruption at the heart of its demands; yet turnout in the 2026 legislative elections fell to 38.08%.
  4. A four-profile model (leave, engage, both, neither), eight testable hypotheses and a comparative Morocco–Europe survey of 18–35-year-olds.

Who this text is for

Researchers in political science, sociology and migration studies, policy-makers, youth organisations and actors in Euro-Mediterranean cooperation.

Abstract

Moroccan youth have three possible responses to a future they see as blocked: leave, speak up or withdraw. In 2025 and 2026 all three became visible at once: a rising intention to emigrate, the GenZ 212 mobilisation and the lowest electoral turnout since 2007. This study treats them as three sides of the same relationship to the future. It reviews the latest institutional evidence, proposes a model combining the aspirations–capabilities theory of migration with Albert O. Hirschman’s exit–voice–loyalty framework, sets out eight testable hypotheses and describes the comparative Morocco–Europe survey designed to test them.

Résumé (français)

La jeunesse marocaine dispose de trois réponses à un horizon jugé bouché : partir, se faire entendre ou se retirer. Les années 2025 et 2026 ont rendu ces trois réponses visibles en même temps : une intention d’émigrer en hausse, la mobilisation GenZ 212, puis la participation électorale la plus faible depuis 2007. Cette étude les analyse comme les trois faces d’un même rapport à l’avenir. Elle établit l’état des connaissances à partir des sources institutionnelles les plus récentes, propose un modèle articulant la théorie des aspirations et capacités migratoires et la triade exit–voice–loyalty d’Albert O. Hirschman, formule huit hypothèses testables et décrit le dispositif d’enquête comparative Maroc–Europe destiné à les éprouver.
